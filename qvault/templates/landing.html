No single person can move the money.

Q-Vault holds a payment, an access grant or any decision until enough of the named people approve it. Each approval is a post-quantum signature over the exact text, and every step goes into a log that anyone can check without trusting us.

How it works

  1. Someone raises a decision

    A payment, an access grant or any other decision, written out in full. Approvers sign that exact text, so a change made afterwards breaks every signature.

  2. The approvers sign it

    On the web or on the phone app, each with their own key. A vault's rule says how many must agree, for example any 2 of 3.

  3. It passes only with enough signatures

    Every signature and every outcome goes into the audit log. Anyone you share a decision with can check it offline.

What it protects, and what it doesn't

With a rule that needs two or more approvers, one person or one stolen password can't approve alone, and changes to the record show. It doesn't stop enough approvers agreeing to something wrong, and it hasn't been independently audited.
