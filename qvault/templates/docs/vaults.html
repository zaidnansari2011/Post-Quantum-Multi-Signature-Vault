A vault is a group of people plus one rule: how many of them must agree before anything in it happens. Decisions live inside a vault and inherit its rule.

Roles

  • Owner — created the vault. Can add and remove members, change roles, and set the threshold. Counts as an approver. There is exactly one, and they cannot be removed.
  • Can approve — signs decisions. These are the people the threshold counts.
  • View only — sees the vault and its decisions but cannot sign. Nothing is ever reported as waiting on them.

The threshold

The threshold is how many approvals a decision needs. It cannot exceed the number of people who can approve — a vault whose threshold nobody could ever meet would deadlock every future decision, so the product refuses that rather than allowing it.

Changing the threshold affects new decisions only. Anything already open keeps the threshold it was created with.

Changing membership

Membership changes are about the vault's future, never its past. Every decision froze its own list of eligible approvers when it opened, so removing someone does not invalidate a signature they already gave, and adding someone does not entitle them to sign a decision that predates them.

That is deliberate. If removing a member could retroactively strip their approval, an administrator could reverse a decision by editing a membership list — which is precisely what the frozen list exists to prevent.

Two changes are refused: removing or demoting the owner, and any change that would leave fewer approvers than the threshold requires.

Files

Each vault has its own ML-KEM key pair, used to protect files attached to its decisions. That private key is held by the server under its master key rather than by any individual, because every member of the vault needs to be able to read the files — a password-locked personal key could not do that. Your signing key is the opposite: only your password opens it.
