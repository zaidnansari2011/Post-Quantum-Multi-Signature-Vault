A vault can have a treasury: a contract on the Sepolia test network that holds ETH and pays out only when enough of the vault's approvers have signed that exact payment. The contract checks their post-quantum signatures itself, so neither this server nor the account that sends the transaction can move the money without them.

Creating one

On the vault's Treasury tab, the owner chooses Create treasury. Nothing is sent from the page: the server registers each approver's key on the network, deploys the contract, waits for the blocks to be final, and checks everything it deployed before the vault shows it as linked. It takes about fifteen minutes, and the tab shows how far it has got. Send ETH to the treasury's address to fund it.

Each approver chooses which of their keys the treasury holds for them, on their account page or from their phone: the password key they use here, or their phone's own key. One key per person: the contract counts keys, so this is what stops one person counting twice.

Paying

StepWhat happens
Raise it Under New decision, choose Payment and give the recipient and the amount. The wording is written from the payment, so what people read and what they sign cannot disagree.
Approve it An approval signs twice with one password or one fingerprint: the vote, as for any decision, and an authorisation over exactly this payment that the contract checks. A phone works both out for itself and refuses to sign if the server's version differs.
Paid Once enough approvals are in, the server sends the payment. The decision's Payout panel shows the transaction, its block and its cost. On the network, the payment carries the decision's own hash, so anyone can match the two.

Approvals expire: a payment decision is open for up to 30 days (7 unless you choose), and has 72 hours after that to be paid. Rejecting works exactly as for any decision and authorises nothing.

When the vault changes

If members join or leave, the approval threshold changes, or someone changes the key the treasury holds for them, the Treasury tab shows the treasury as out of date and lists who joins and leaves. The owner asks for the change; the treasury's current approvers approve it, as they would a payment, and then it is made on the network. The server never changes who controls the money by itself.

Two consequences to expect. Payments cannot be raised while a change is in progress. And a change cancels every payment approval collected before it: those payments have to be raised and approved again, because approvals given to one group of people are not carried over to another.

What this does not promise

  • It is a test network. The on-chain verifier is new and has not been independently audited, so no real money should sit behind it.
  • An approval made with a password key is produced by this server, exactly as a password-key vote is. The guarantee that what you approved is what gets paid holds end to end for approvals made on a phone.
  • If fewer approvers than the threshold can ever sign again, the treasury cannot be changed and its funds stay where they are. The app warns before any change that would cause this.
