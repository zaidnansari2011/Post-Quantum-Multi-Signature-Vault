A decision is something you want done that nobody should be able to do alone: releasing a payment, approving a contract, granting emergency access. You write it down, and a set number of people have to sign it before it counts.

Proposing

Anyone in a vault can propose a decision. You give it a title and the exact wording of the action being authorised. At that moment four things are frozen together and hashed: the wording, the approval threshold, the list of people eligible to approve, and a random value that makes the decision unique.

Because those are frozen, changing the wording afterwards — or adding and removing members — cannot retroactively change what anyone agreed to. If the stored text is edited directly in the database, the decision page says so and stops counting the signatures on it.

Signing

Approvers type their password, which unlocks their private signing key for that one operation. The signature commits to three things at once: which decision, what was decided, and who decided it. Because all three are signed together, a signature cannot be moved onto a different decision, flipped from reject to approve, or attributed to somebody else.

Each approver signs once. That is enforced by a uniqueness constraint in the database, not just by the interface.

The threshold

A vault set to “2 of 3” needs two of its three approvers. The tally counts only signatures that verify at the moment you look — every signature is re-checked on every page load. A tampered signature row silently stops counting rather than manufacturing an approval.

A decision becomes approved when it reaches its threshold, and rejected once enough people have refused that the threshold can no longer be reached. If it has a deadline and that passes first, it expires without effect.

Attachments

A decision can carry a file. It is encrypted with AES-256-GCM before it is stored, and the key for it is wrapped for the vault using ML-KEM. The file never reaches disk in the clear, and the server checks it has not been modified before handing it back.
