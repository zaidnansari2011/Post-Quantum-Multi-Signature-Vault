14 October 2026: the Recovery Kit

  • A Recovery Kit: a code you print or download and keep offline. If you forget your password, it lets you choose a new one and keep your signing key. You're shown it once when you sign up, and you can make a new one on the Security page.
  • Using a kit starts a 72-hour wait, and nothing changes until it ends. You, your workspace's owners and admins and your fellow approvers are told at once, and any of you can cancel it. Signing in with your password cancels it too.
  • Anyone with your kit and your email address can do the same, so keep it where you keep your passport. If you cancel a recovery you didn't start, that kit stops working; make a new one.
  • Notices about a recovery can't be archived or marked read while it's open, and Home shows it with a Cancel button until it ends.
  • In a vault where whoever raises a decision can't approve it, a decision now ends as rejected once the people who can vote can no longer pass it, and whoever raised it is told.

9 October 2026: decisions in more depth, and email

  • Decision types: General, Production access and Contract, each with its own fields, which write the text everyone signs.
  • A vault can say that whoever raises a decision can't approve it. New vaults start that way.
  • Before you raise a decision you see who can approve it and how many approvals it needs. A decision that could never pass can't be raised.
  • Rejecting asks for a reason. Whoever raised a decision can withdraw it, or raise a closed one again.
  • Each decision has a discussion, where you can mention people. It isn't part of what is signed.
  • Changes to a vault's rule are shown as before and after, on the vault and in the audit log.
  • Notifications can also come by email, when the server has email set up. An email never carries an amount, an address or an approve button: it links to the decision.

9 October 2026: sign-up and the public pages

  • Signing up creates your own workspace, with you as its owner. The only way into someone else's workspace is their invitation link.
  • Sign-up says plainly that your password also unlocks your signing key and can't be reset, and asks you to confirm you've read that.
  • A forgot-password page that says what can and can't be done.
  • Signing up never makes anyone an administrator of the whole system.
  • Repeated attempts to sign in, sign up or pair a phone from one address are refused for a while, and so are repeated wrong passwords when you sign or change your password.
  • New pages: Security, Status, Pricing and this changelog. The front page shows the live log head and whether the witness is current.

8 October 2026: every screen redesigned

  • Every screen uses the new design, in light and dark.
  • New date, file and checkbox controls. A refused form marks the field that needs fixing and moves the focus to it.
  • Every control has a name a screen reader can read, and controls are larger on touch screens.

6 October 2026: work first, workspaces and notifications

  • Home shows what needs your signature, what's due soon and what's waiting on others.
  • Approvals has tabs: Needs your signature, Waiting on others, Expiring soon and Done.
  • A decision's page has three layers: Overview, Evidence and Technical. Each decision has a short code, such as 9E89-97A2, to compare across screens.
  • Workspaces: members with roles (owner, admin, member, auditor), invitations by link, suspending and reinstating, and a getting-started checklist.
  • Notifications in the app: the bell, an inbox, reminders while a decision waits on you, a warning a day before it's due, and preferences. Email and push aren't built yet.
  • Choose a light, dark or system theme; it's remembered for your account.
